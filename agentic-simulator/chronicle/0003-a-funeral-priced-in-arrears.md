# Week 3 — A Funeral Priced in Arrears

**CHRONICLE OF RURAVAQAN — WEEK THE THIRD**

The council granted the Mint its petition by a narrow margin: House Ferrand's debt was ranked senior to any inheritance claim, and the customs revenue attached to it was assigned to the Mint before any inheritance could be settled. The Bastard contested the ranking and lost. Stung or unstung, the Bastard made for the Doge's manor the same week, timing the attempt to a gap in the Mint's watcher rotation, with the intent to steal or destroy the instrument on which House Ferrand's claim rests. The gap existed. The opportunity did not. The Mint's watchers foiled the attempt, and the instrument ended the week exactly where it began.

At the Reliquary, the tithe ledger was read aloud from the pulpit — every debtor family, every sum, down to the copper — and interdiction was declared: no rites and no consecrated burial for any debtor house until arrears were paid. The Doge's public funeral was appointed for the single day House Ferrand should settle in full. The city's principal ceremony became, in effect, a receipt.

The Marsh Gang tested the arrangement by night, striking at the Reliquary's storerooms for the funeral wealth held there. The Reliquary's answer was decisive. The raid concluded without profit, and the storerooms kept what the pulpit had counted.

At the harbor, the longshoremen struck and carried: no loading, no unloading, no customs inspections, until the council paid the fleet venture's back wages in full. The strike took by the thinnest of margins, and held.

The week's bookkeeping thus closed on itself. The Mint had won revenue the harbor no longer produced; the longshoremen held the quay that would have produced it; the funeral waited on House Ferrand; and the Bastard waited on everything.
